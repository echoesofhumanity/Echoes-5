## 1. Binding organizational principles
- Twenty-five equal council seats, including two co-chairs. One institutional vote per verified seat; no co-chair veto or unilateral constitutional/budgetary power.
- The council alone sets constitution, global institutional policy, consolidated budgets, strategic commitments and institution-wide delegation rules.
- Clusters (salkımlar) have **real delegated operational decision-making authority** within a council-approved policy, budget, safety protocol and mandate. Routine operational decisions do not need a council vote.
- A council seat coordinates its assigned cluster but does not gain unlimited control over experts, money, protected information or appeals.
- Invited coordinators, experts, interpreters and cluster representatives can advise or speak when authorized; they never gain institutional voting rights.
- Volunteer governance; no automatic salary, donation demand or personal entitlement to operating revenue. Expense reimbursements require independent approval and legal/accounting review.
- No real cases, real commerce, binding votes or protected identities in public GitHub Pages pilots.

## 2. Three organizational layers
1. **Global Council (25 seats)**: global strategy, policies, constitution, budgets, accountability, appointment framework.
2. **Seat stewardship (25 accountable portfolios)**: coordinates the activity portfolio, reports risk and results, prepares institutional motions, delegates operational mandates; does not replace collective governance.
3. **Operational clusters**: trained coordinators, reviewers, educators, producers and digital citizens exercise authorized routine operational judgment and keep an auditable record. Their internal structure may vary by domain.

## 4. Decision routing
**Operational:** a decision stays within a cluster when (a) it follows existing policy, (b) spending is inside a preapproved limit, (c) qualified authority and safety checks exist, (d) it does not change institution-wide rights or rules, and (e) no serious unresolved conflict of interest exists. A cluster records rationale, accountable role, date, and any required second review.

**Institutional:** route to the council when changing policy, constitution, global budget, high-value commitments, permanent cross-cluster mandates, material risk appetite or binding rights/obligations. A proposal from a cluster is an agenda item, not an automatic vote.

**Stop/escalate:** unresolved child protection, urgent threats, protected data misuse, major financial anomalies, conflicts of interest or out-of-mandate decisions trigger specialist safeguarding/legal response and temporary restriction as appropriate. Emergency assistance requires real approved partners and protocols; this document does not authorize dispatch.

## 5. Responsibility and checks
- **Responsible:** delegated coordinator executes an operational decision.
- **Accountable:** portfolio seat monitors scope, evidence and outcome; cannot waive independent controls.
- **Consulted:** adjacent portfolios and domain experts where required.
- **Independent reviewer:** privacy, legal, safeguarding or finance reviewer with no conflicting personal interest.
- **Informed:** council receives periodic aggregated reports; sensitive case details remain in a separate protected system.

Examples:
- Marketplace producer/product review: seat 13 cluster decides under published acceptance criteria; seat 22 handles legal concerns; seat 15 checks commercial controls. New global acceptance policy goes to council.
- Academy class scheduling: seat 07 cluster decides within approved educational framework; seat 06 oversees curriculum, seat 03 safeguards minors. New worldwide curriculum policy goes to council.
- Media publication: seats 09–12 manage their own editorial schedules and rights checks. New institution-wide editorial charter goes to council.
- Financial reporting: seat 24 prepares records; no individual may alone propose, approve, pay and reconcile the same transaction.

## 6. Meeting chamber vs operational workspace
**Meeting chamber:** verified seat identity, agenda, translated canonical motion, quorum rules once ratified, one vote per eligible seat, independent audit and certified decision. Conference moderator access never implies voting access.
**Cluster workspace:** delegated tasks, decision log, progress, evidence, review and escalation; no institutional vote button.
**Cross-link:** institutional proposal may be sent to meeting agenda only through an explicit review step; no automatic promotion of a task into a vote.
